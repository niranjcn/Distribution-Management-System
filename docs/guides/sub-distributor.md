# Sub Distributor Guide

**Who you are:** As **Sub Distributor** you run distribution operations in the field.
You receive devices from headquarters, distribute them down to the clusters and operators
under you, confirm every delivery that arrives, report defects and confirm replacement
receipts, watch returns and pending payments, and grow your team by creating the users
who work under your sub-distribution. Your workspace is the **Sub Distributor Dashboard**,
scoped to your area and the clusters/operators beneath you.

You are the link in the chain **PDIC → Sub Distributor → Cluster → Operator**: everything
you receive is handed further down once you confirm it, so confirming deliveries promptly
keeps the whole chain moving.

Read **[getting-started.md](getting-started.md)** first for the login basics and the
layout (sidebar on the left, notification bell and profile menu top-right).

## 1. Managing your profile & credentials

**Purpose:** keep your own account accurate — your details, email, phone, and password are
all managed from your **Profile** page. When you log in for the first time (or when
headquarters marks your account for an update), you are asked to set a new email and/or
password before entering the site — do that once and then continue.

### Step-by-step: change your details later (Profile)

1. Click the **profile menu** in the **top-right corner** of the screen.
2. Click **Profile** from the dropdown.
3. You will see two tabs at the top of the page: **Profile Information** and **Security**.

**Edit your profile details (name, designation, address, pincode):**

1. Stay on the **Profile Information** tab (or click it if you are on Security).
2. Edit the **Full Name**, **Designation**, **Address**, and **Pincode** fields.
3. Click **Save Changes** at the bottom right. A green "Profile updated successfully"
   message confirms it.

### Change your email or phone

1. Click the **Security** tab.
2. In the **Email Address** card, see your **Current Email** (greyed out) and type the
   **New Email** in the box, then click **Change Email**.
3. In the **Phone Number** card, type the new number (min 10 digits) in the
   **New Phone Number** box, then click **Change Phone**.

### Change your password

1. On the **Security** tab, find the **Password** card.
2. Enter **Current Password** (your existing password).
3. Enter **New Password** (at least 6 characters) and **Confirm New Password**.
4. Click **Update Password**. A green message confirms it.

> **Want a different credential changed?** Your profile lets you change your own details
> any time. If an account under you ever needs a credential change that is outside your
> scope, contact headquarters so they can apply it.

## 2. Your dashboard

Your **Sub Distributor Dashboard** is the first screen after login and gives you a
real-time pulse of your area. Everything on it is scoped to you and the clusters and
operators under your sub-distribution.

Use the **date range filter** (top right) to focus the numbers — Today, Last 7 / 30 / 60 /
90 days, This Year, All Time, or a **Custom Range**. Setting it first makes every number
below match the period you care about.

### The headline numbers

Six cards summarise your area:

- **Received Devices** — how many devices you have in hand.
- **Pending Confirmations** — deliveries sent to you that you still need to confirm.
- **My Operators** — how many operators work under your sub-distribution.
- **Defect Reports** — defect reports in your area.
- **Returns** — return records in your area.
- **Distributions** — how many distributions you have sent.

### Devices under your chain

A three-tile card shows how your inventory is spread:

- **Total Devices** — everything in your sub-distribution.
- **Total Devices In Hand** — the ones physically with you.
- **Under the Chain** — the devices held by your clusters and operators.

### Charts

Two doughnut charts give an at-a-glance health check:

- **My Device Active vs Inactive** — your devices by active/inactive.
- **Cluster/Operator Account Active vs Inactive** — your team accounts by active/inactive.

### The pending receipt banner

If any delivery is waiting for your confirmation, an orange banner appears at the top:
*"You have N delivery(ies) to confirm"*, with a **Confirm Now** button that jumps straight
to **Delivery Confirmations** (see section 11). Confirm these promptly — the devices stay
with the sender until you confirm receipt.

### Quick cards

- **My Devices** — your latest devices with their status; **View all** opens the Devices
  page.
- **Pending Confirmations** — the deliveries waiting for you with a **Confirm Receipt**
  button on each row.
- **My Operators** — your operators with their status; **View all** opens User Management.
- **Defect Reports to Review** — open defect reports in your area.
- **Return Requests** — recent return records.

You can also click **Assign to Operator** (top right) to jump straight into creating a
distribution.

## 3. Finding a device (Track Device)

**Purpose:** the **Track Device** page is where you look up any device and see exactly
where it is and everywhere it has been. It appears in your sidebar.

### Step-by-step: search for a specific device

1. Open **Track Device** from the sidebar.
2. In the search box at the top, type the device's **serial number**, **NUID**, or
   **MAC address**.
3. Click **Track Device** (or press Enter).

### What you see in the result

- **Device Info card** — manufacturer, MAC, serial, device type, and device ID.
- **Current Location** — shows the holder type and who holds it now (for example
  "Sub Distributor — Ajay Traders"). Colours help: PDIC (blue), Sub Distributor
  (purple), Cluster (indigo), Operator (green).
- **Availability Status** — the current status (available, distributed, in use,
  defective, returned, etc.).
- **Device Journey** — a timeline of every action on the device (registered, distributed
  from person A → person B, status changes). The most recent entry is marked **current**.
- **Distribution Flow** — a visual chain: **PDIC → Sub Distributor → Cluster → Operator**.
  The highlighted step shows where the device currently sits.

### Step-by-step: find a device when you don't know its serial

Before you search, the page lists the devices you can see (active devices first). To
narrow the list:

1. Click **Show Filters** (top of the device list).
2. Use the dropdowns to filter by **Device Type**, **Vendor / manufacturer**, **Status**,
   and the **Sub Distribution / Cluster** filters (to narrow to a region).
3. Click a device card to open its full journey.

### Using Track Device efficiently

- **Always confirm your deliveries** — after you confirm receipt, open the device here to
  see it land with you as the current holder.
- **Use the journey for hand-offs** — if you are about to distribute devices, check the
  device is actually with you first (its journey's last stop should be you).
- **Investigate a dispute** — when a delivery is disputed, the journey shows exactly where
  the devices last were, which helps you and the sender work out what happened.

## 4. Using reports efficiently

You have two reporting pages — **Cluster Report** and **Operator Report** — both scoped to
your sub-distribution. They work the same way, and together they give you a top-down view
of your area.

### The shared controls

- **Date range filter** (top-right): Today, Last 7 / 30 / 60 / 90 days, This Year, All
  Time, or **Custom Range** (pick start and end dates, then **Apply**). Set this first —
  every number on the page updates to match.
- **View tabs: Total / SB / ONT** — switch between all devices, SB devices, and ONT
  devices. In Total you see combined counts; in SB and ONT you see a per-vendor breakdown.
- **Filter Report** bar: choose which field to search (**All Fields, Name, Email, Phone,
  Digital ID, Broadband ID**), type a value, and click **Search**. Use **Reset** to clear.
- **Export PDF** (top right) saves the current view, and **Download** pulls the data file.

### How to use the Cluster Report

This answers *"which clusters sit under me, and how much do they hold?"*

1. Set your date range.
2. Read each cluster's **Total Operators** and its **Device Count / SB / ONT / Other**
   breakdown.
3. Use the **SB / ONT** tabs to see per-vendor numbers for that type.
4. Use **Filter Report** to jump to one cluster by name or ID.

### How to use the Operator Report

This answers *"who holds what, down to the last operator?"*

1. Set your date range.
2. Use the **Sub Distribution** and **Cluster** dropdowns to narrow to a region (the
   Cluster dropdown becomes active once you pick a Sub Distribution).
3. Each row shows the operator, their **digital/broadband IDs**, their parent cluster,
   and their device counts.
4. Use **Filter Report** to search an individual operator by name or digital ID.

### Efficiency tips

- **Always set the date range first** — otherwise you are looking at All Time and the
  tables are large.
- **Work top-down:** Cluster Report → Operator Report, keeping the same region selected,
  to drill down without guessing.
- **Use tabs to isolate SB vs ONT** when investigating a vendor complaint.
- **Search beats scrolling** — for a single cluster or operator, use the Filter Report
  search rather than paging through rows.

## 6. User management

**Purpose:** grow and organise your team. As Sub Distributor you can **create** users who
work under your sub-distribution and view everyone in your hierarchy.

### 6.1 My Users

Open **User Management → My Users** from the sidebar.

- The page shows **clusters and operators under your sub-distribution**, with summary
  cards up top.
- Use **Add User** (top right) to create a new account.
- The roles you can create are **Sub Distribution MD/Manager**, **Cluster**, **Operator**,
  and **Sub Distribution Employee**.
- **Search** by name, email, role, phone, digital ID, or broadband ID, then click the
  **eye** on any row to open the account details.

### 6.2 Step-by-step: create a user (Add User)

1. Open **My Users** and click **Add User**.
2. Fill in the required fields:
   - **Full Name** — the person's name.
   - **Email** — their login email.
   - **Password** and **Confirm Password** — at least 6 characters.
   - **Role** — pick one of the roles you can create (see 6.1).
3. Set the **parent / assignment** for the new account:
   - Creating a **Cluster** or **Sub Distribution MD/Manager** → assign it to your
     sub-distribution (pre-selected for you).
   - Creating an **Operator** → choose whether they report directly to you or to one of
     your clusters, then select the parent.
4. For **Cluster** and **Operator** roles you can also add **Digital / Broadband IDs**
   (the primary ID is the first row; use **+** to add more). Operators can have a
   **Network Name**.
5. Click **Create**. A green message confirms it and the account appears in your list.

### 6.3 User Hierarchy

Open **User Management → User Hierarchy** — labelled **My User Hierarchy** for you.

- See your team as a **visual tree**: your clusters, and the operators under each cluster
  (and directly under you).
- A **legend** shows the colour for each role, and the **search box** filters the tree.
- Use **Add User** to create an account right from the tree — same form as 6.2.

### 6.4 Step-by-step: bulk upload users

Use this when creating several accounts at once.

1. Open **User Management → Bulk Upload**.
2. Pick a **mode**:
   - **Clusters (under me)** — create clusters under your sub-distribution.
   - **Operators (assign to me)** — create operators reporting directly to you.
   - **Operators (under Cluster)** — create operators under one of your clusters (then
     select the cluster).
3. Click **Download Template** to get the CSV template, fill it in, and save.
4. Drop the file into the upload box (or click to browse), then click **Upload**.
5. Read the result: **Created** (green), **Warnings** (yellow), **Errors** (red — with
   the row number and message). Fix any error rows and re-upload only those.

### 6.5 Using user management efficiently

- **Create the whole cluster first, then its operators** — operators placed under a
  cluster need the cluster to exist, so build top-down.
- **Use the tree** to see at a glance who reports where before creating new users — it
  avoids assigning an operator to the wrong parent.
- **Bulk upload for onboarding** — when adding many operators at once, the CSV upload is
  much faster than creating them one by one.

## 9. The distribution workflow

**Purpose:** distributions are how devices move down the chain —
**PDIC → Sub Distributor → Cluster → Operator**. Every distribution you receive is
followed by a **Delivery Confirmation** task; every distribution you send triggers one for
your recipient.

### 9.1 How devices flow to the field

1. **Headquarters sends you a distribution** — PDIC/management hands devices down to you.
   The distribution starts in **Awaiting Receipt** (`pending_receipt`).
2. **You are notified** — a warning notification ("Action Required: Confirm Device
   Receipt") points you to the **Delivery Confirmations** page.
3. **You confirm or dispute**:
   - **Received** → the distribution becomes **Confirmed**; the devices are now yours and
     you can distribute them further down.
   - **Not Received** → the distribution becomes **Disputed**; management and the sender
     are alerted immediately.
4. **You distribute to your team** — send devices to a **Cluster** or **Operator** under
   your sub-distribution. They then confirm receipt, and the device is theirs.

> **The golden rule:** devices stay with the sender until the recipient confirms receipt.
> Only after confirmation can the recipient redistribute them further down the chain.

### 9.2 Your place in the workflow

- **As recipient:** confirm every delivery that arrives to you — this unlocks the devices
  so you can hand them onward.
- **As sender:** create distributions to your clusters and operators, then wait for their
  confirmation. If a recipient disputes, coordinate so the devices come back.
- **Disputed returns are resolved by management** — when a delivery to you is disputed and
  the devices are back with the sender, management marks the return complete; your part is
  to confirm receipt promptly so disputes are rare.

### 9.3 The statuses, explained

| Status | What it means | Who acts |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Awaiting Receipt** (`pending_receipt`) | Sent; the recipient hasn't confirmed yet. Devices are still effectively the sender's. | Recipient confirms or disputes |
| **Confirmed** (`approved`) | Recipient confirmed receipt. Devices now belong to them and can be redistributed. | Recipient (already done) |
| **Disputed** (`disputed`) | Recipient says the devices did **not** arrive. You, the sender, and management are notified. | Recipient / management |
| Cancelled | The creator cancelled the request before it was resolved. | Creator |
| Rejected | Management closed the distribution without accepting it (e.g. after a dispute is resolved). | Management |

## 10. Managing distributions

**Purpose:** create and track distributions from your sub-distribution down to your
clusters and operators. You have **My Distributions**, **Create Distribution**, and
**Bulk Upload** under the **Distribution** menu.

### 10.1 Step-by-step: create a distribution to a cluster or operator

1. Open **Distribution → Create Distribution** (or click **Assign to Operator** on your
   dashboard).
2. On the left, **select the devices** to send. Use the **Selection Mode** buttons:
   - **Manual** — click devices one by one.
   - **Registered Date / Date Range / Serial Range** — pick a window and the matching
     devices appear.
   - **Search box** — type MAC, model, or serial to filter the list.
   - **Select Filtered (N)** — grabs every device matching your current filter at once.
3. Check the **Selected Devices** panel on the right; remove any by clicking the trash
   icon on the row.
4. Under **Select Recipient**:
   - Pick the **Recipient Type** — as Sub Distributor you can send to a **Cluster** or
     an **Operator** under you.
   - Use the **Cluster filter** to narrow to one cluster's operators if needed, then pick
     the **final recipient**. A breadcrumb shows the path
     (e.g. `Sub Distributor → Cluster → Recipient`).
5. Set the **Date of Distribution** (leave blank for today) and add **Notes** if needed.
6. Click **Create Distribution**. Review the summary in the confirm dialog and click
   **Confirm Transfer**.
7. A green "Distribution created successfully!" message appears and you return to the
   list, where the new row shows **Awaiting Receipt**.

### 10.2 Step-by-step: bulk upload a distribution

Use this when sending a large, known set of devices.

1. Open **Distribution → Bulk Upload**.
2. Click **Download CSV Template**. Columns: `mac_address, serial_number, nuid` —
   provide **any one** per row (a row can also have more than one).
3. Fill the file and save. Supported: `.xlsx`, `.xls`, `.csv`.
4. **Select Recipient** the same way as in a single create (type → cluster filter →
   recipient).
5. **Drop the file** into the upload box (or click to browse). Add optional **Notes** and
   a **Date of Distribution**.
6. Click **Upload & Create Distribution**.
7. Read the result: **Rows Processed, Valid Devices, Errors**. Invalid/unregistered rows
   block the whole upload — fix the listed rows and re-upload.

### 10.3 Step-by-step: view and analyse your distributions

1. Open **Distribution → My Distributions**. The cards at the top give a snapshot:
   **Total, Awaiting Receipt, Confirmed, Disputed**.
2. Set the **date range** (top right) to focus on a period.
3. Use **Search Distributions**: pick a field (**All Fields, Distribution ID, From, To,
   Digital ID, Broadband ID, Status, Confirmed By**), type, and **Search**.
4. Click a row (or the **eye** icon) to open **Distribution Details**:
   - Sender → recipient, status badge, and **By Device Type / By Vendor** breakdowns.
   - **Total Sent, SB count, Total Devices** summary boxes.
   - Confirmed By, transfer date, and any notes.

### 10.4 Downloading the device list

1. Open the **Distribution Details** of the row you care about.
2. Click **Download CSV** or **Download Excel** (top-right of the details header) to
   export the distribution's devices as **MAC / NUID** data.
3. For the deliveries **you receive**, this download is available to you directly — handy
   for checking a shipment against what arrived.

### 10.5 Approaching distribution work

- **Confirm incoming first, then send onward** — a device you have not confirmed is still
  the sender's. Work your **Pending Confirmations** before creating new distributions.
- **Send to the lowest level that fits** — if an operator needs the device, send it to the
  operator; if a cluster manages a set of operators, send to the cluster so it can
  allocate.
- **Keep the breadcrumb path straight** — always send to a recipient *under you*; the
  system scopes the recipient list to your sub-distribution.
- **Use the dashboard** — your **Pending Confirmations** and **Distributions** cards show
  where attention is needed before you plan the next batch.

## 11. Delivery Confirmations

**Purpose:** this page is where every delivery **to you** gets confirmed or disputed.
It is the moment a transfer becomes final.

### 11.1 Step-by-step: confirm or dispute a delivery

1. Open **Delivery Confirmations** from the sidebar.
2. The list shows distributions awaiting your receipt (`pending_receipt`), each with the
   sender, distribution ID, device count, and status.
3. For a delivery that arrived, click **Confirm Receipt**:
   - The distribution becomes **Confirmed**, the devices become yours, and you can now
     redistribute them.
   - Add any notes if useful, then confirm.
4. For a delivery that did **not** arrive, choose **Dispute** — management and the sender
   are alerted immediately.

> **Why confirming matters:** a device that isn't confirmed stays with the sender and
> blocks redistribution. Confirm every delivery that physically arrives, as soon as it
> arrives.

### 11.2 Step-by-step: confirming from the Distribution details

1. Open **My Distributions** and open the awaiting row.
2. In **Distribution Details**, click **Confirm Receipt** in the footer.
3. Confirm the device count matches what you received, then confirm.

## 12. Defect Reports

**Purpose:** when a device fails, the cycle is: you **report the defect** → management
**reviews** it → the device is returned → a **replacement** is assigned → you **confirm**
the replacement physically arrives.

### 12.1 The defect workflow (read this first)

1. **You report a defect** — select the defective device, describe the issue, add a photo
   if available, and submit. The device is marked **defective** and management is alerted.
2. **Management reviews** the report and approves or rejects it.
   - **Approve** → a **return request is automatically created** and you are told to
     return the defective device.
   - **Reject** → the report is closed.
3. **The device comes back** — you send the defective device back; management confirms it
   reached PDIC. Only then is a replacement possible.
4. **A replacement is assigned** — management picks an existing device, reuses the same
   (serviced) device, or registers a new one. The defect moves to
   **replacement_pending_confirmation**.
5. **You confirm the replacement** physically — it becomes active in your account only
   after you click **Confirm** (see 12.3).

### 12.2 Step-by-step: report a defect

1. Open **Defect Reports** from the sidebar.
2. Click **Report a Defect** (or **create**).
3. **Select the device** — search or pick the defective device from the ones in your area.
4. Fill in the details:
   - **Defect Type** and **Severity**.
   - A clear **Description** of the problem.
   - Attach a **photo** if available.
5. Submit. The device is marked defective and management is notified to review it.

### 12.3 Step-by-step: confirm a replacement receipt

1. Open **Defect Reports**. A report in **replacement_pending_confirmation** that was
   reported by you (or is on a device you hold) shows a green **Confirm** action.
2. Only after the replacement device **physically arrives**, click **Confirm**.
3. The defect becomes **resolved** and the replacement device is **activated in your
   account** (you'll see a "Replacement Receipt Confirmed" notification).

> **The golden rule:** confirm a replacement **only after physically receiving the
> device**. Confirming early would mark the replacement active before it actually reached
> you.

### 12.4 The Defect Reports page, in detail

- **Summary cards** — Total, Open, Under Review, Resolved, Critical.
- **Search** — by Report ID, Device Serial, NUID, Description, Defect Type, Severity,
  Status, Reported By, Digital ID, Broadband ID, or Device Type. Use **Reset** to clear.
- **Table columns** — Device, Type, Severity, Reported By, Date, Status, **Payment
  Status** (Confirmed / Pending Payment), Actions.
- **View filters** — **All / Pending Replacement / Replaced** narrow the list to
  defect–replacement work.
- **Row actions** — the **eye** opens full details (device, severity, description, the
  Defective/Replacement Device boxes, any auto-created return ID, and photos); the
  **Confirm** action appears on replacements waiting for your receipt.

## 13. Pending Payments & Replacements

### 13.1 The Pending Payments page

**Purpose:** **Pending Payments** shows the **payment dues** owed in your area. A due
exists when a returned defective device is **received**, has a **return amount above
zero**, and the **payment is not yet confirmed**.

1. Open **Pending Payments** from the sidebar.
2. Summary cards show **Users (count), Total Due, Total Items** — the outstanding picture
   across your area.
3. The table groups dues **per user**: each row shows the user name, role, parent,
   digital/broadband IDs, number of due items, and total due (highest first).
4. **Search** by user name, role, parent name, digital ID, broadband ID, or total due.
5. Click a user row to open a **details drawer**: that user's defects with report ID,
   device, return ID, return status, amount, and received date.

> **Your view:** this is how you stay aware of what is owed in your area. Payment
> confirmation is handled at headquarters, so your focus here is monitoring and sharing
> the list when you follow up with a user.

### 13.2 The Replacements pages

**Purpose:** replacement records track each **defective device → replacement device**
mapping. The **Replacements** menu appears when there are **received returns at the
office**.

1. **Replacements** — the list of replacement records: the defective device, its
   replacement, and the mapping. Search and open rows for details.
2. **Pending Replacements** (`/replacements/pending`) — approved defects that still need a
   replacement device, split into **awaiting / ready / waiting** counts. This is where
   you can see whether a replacement for your area is waiting to ship.

### 13.3 Replacement Confirmation

When a replacement has been assigned to you and is ready, the **Replacement Confirmation**
page (and the **Confirm** action on the Defect Reports page) is where you complete the
hand-off:

1. Open **Replacement Confirmation** from the sidebar (it shows when there is replacement
   work for you).
2. See the replacement waiting for your confirmation.
3. Confirm **only after the device physically arrives**. The replacement becomes active in
   your account and the defect is resolved.
