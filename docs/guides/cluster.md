# Cluster Guide

**Who you are:** As **Cluster** you manage the devices and operators of one cluster in the
field. You receive devices from your sub-distributor, distribute them to the operators in
your cluster, confirm every delivery that arrives, report defects and confirm replacement
receipts, watch returns and pending payments, and create the operators who work under
your cluster. Your workspace is the **Cluster Dashboard**, scoped to your cluster.

You are the link in the chain **PDIC → Sub Distributor → Cluster → Operator**: everything
you receive is handed to your operators once you confirm it, so confirming deliveries
promptly keeps the whole chain moving.

Read **[getting-started.md](getting-started.md)** first for the login basics and the
layout (sidebar on the left, notification bell and profile menu top-right).

## 1. Managing your profile & credentials

**Purpose:** keep your own account accurate — your details, email, phone, and password
are all managed from your **Profile** page. When you log in for the first time (or when
headquarters marks your account for an update), you are asked to set a new email and/or
password before entering the site — do that once and then continue.

### Step-by-step: change your details later (Profile)

1. Click the **profile menu** in the **top-right corner** of the screen.
2. Click **Profile** from the dropdown.
3. You will see two tabs at the top of the page: **Profile Information** and **Security**.

**Edit your profile details (name, designation, address, pincode):**

1. Stay on the **Profile Information** tab (or click it if you are on Security).
2. Edit the **Full Name**, **Designation**, **Address**, and **Pincode** fields.
3. Click **Save Changes** at the bottom right. A green "Profile updated successfully"
   message confirms it.

### Change your email or phone

1. Click the **Security** tab.
2. In the **Email Address** card, see your **Current Email** (greyed out) and type the
   **New Email** in the box, then click **Change Email**.
3. In the **Phone Number** card, type the new number (min 10 digits) in the
   **New Phone Number** box, then click **Change Phone**.

### Change your password

1. On the **Security** tab, find the **Password** card.
2. Enter **Current Password** (your existing password).
3. Enter **New Password** (at least 6 characters) and **Confirm New Password**.
4. Click **Update Password**. A green message confirms it.

> **Want a different credential changed?** Your profile lets you change your own details
> any time. If an account under you ever needs a credential change that is outside your
> scope, contact your sub-distributor so the management team can apply it.

## 2. Your dashboard

Your **Cluster Dashboard** is the first screen after login and gives you a real-time
pulse of your cluster. Everything on it is scoped to you and the operators under you.

Use the **date range filter** (top right) to focus the numbers — Today, Last 7 / 30 / 60
/ 90 days, This Year, All Time, or a **Custom Range**. Setting it first makes every
number below match the period you care about.

### The headline numbers

Six cards summarise your cluster:

- **Devices** — how many devices you hold in hand.
- **Pending Confirmations** — deliveries sent to you that you still need to confirm.
- **My Operators** — how many operators work under your cluster.
- **Defect Reports** — defect reports in your cluster.
- **Returns** — return records in your area.
- **Distributions** — how many distributions you have sent.

### Devices under you

A three-tile card shows how your inventory is spread:

- **Total Devices** — everything in your cluster.
- **Total Devices In Hand** — the ones physically with you.
- **Under the Chain** — the devices held by your operators.

### Charts

Two doughnut charts give an at-a-glance health check:

- **My Device Active vs Inactive** — your devices by active/inactive.
- **Operator Account Active vs Inactive** — your operator accounts by active/inactive.

### The pending receipt banner

If any delivery is waiting for your confirmation, an orange banner appears at the top:
*"You have N delivery(ies) to confirm"*, with a **Confirm Now** button that jumps
straight to **Delivery Confirmations** (section 10). Confirm these promptly — the devices
stay with the sender until you confirm receipt.

### Quick cards

- **My Devices** — your recent devices with their status; **View all** opens the Devices
  page.
- **Pending Confirmations** — the deliveries waiting with a **Confirm Receipt** button.
- **My Operators** — your operators with their status; **View all** opens User Management.
- **Defect Reports to Review** — open defect reports in your cluster.
- **Return Requests** — recent return records.

You can also click **Assign to Operator** (top right) to jump straight into creating a
distribution.

## 3. Finding a device (Track Device)

**Purpose:** the **Track Device** page is where you look up any device and see exactly
where it is and everywhere it has been.

### Step-by-step: search for a specific device

1. Open **Track Device** from the sidebar.
2. In the search box at the top, type the device's **serial number**, **NUID**, or
   **MAC address**.
3. Click **Track Device** (or press Enter).

### What you see in the result

- **Device Info card** — manufacturer, MAC, serial, device type, and device ID.
- **Current Location** — shows the holder type and who holds it now (for example
  "Operator — Ram Kumar"). Colours help: Sub Distributor (purple), Cluster (indigo),
  Operator (green).
- **Availability Status** — the current status (available, distributed, in use,
  defective, returned, etc.).
- **Device Journey** — a timeline of every action (registered, sent person A → person B,
  status changes), with the most recent entry marked **current**.
- **Distribution Flow** — the chain **PDIC → Sub Distributor → Cluster → Operator** with
  the current step highlighted.

### Step-by-step: find a device when you don't know its serial

1. After opening Track Device, the page lists recent devices. Use **Show Filters** (top
   of the list) to filter by **Device Type**, **Manufacturer**, **Status**,
   **Sub Distribution**, and **Cluster** to narrow the area.
2. Click any device card to open its full journey.

### Using Track Device efficiently

- **Always confirm your deliveries** — after you confirm, search the device here to see
  it land with you as the current holder.
- **Use the journey for hand-offs** — when you distribute to an operator, the journey
  shows the device left you and reached them, so you can verify every step.
- **Keep filters narrow** — when searching a region, pre-filter by cluster rather than
  scrolling a full list.

## 4. Using reports efficiently

You have one reporting page — the **Operator Report** — scoped to your cluster.

### The shared controls

- **Date range filter** (top right): Today, Last 7 / 30 / 60 / 90 days, This Year, All
  Time, or **Custom Range**. Set it first — every number on the page updates.
- **View tabs (Total / SB / ONT)** — switch between all devices, SB devices, and ONT
  devices. In SB/ONT you see the per-vendor split.
- **Filter Report** — search by **All Fields, Name, Email, Phone, Digital ID, Broadband
  ID**, then click **Search** (use **Reset** to clear).
- **Export PDF** saves the current view; **Download** exports the data file.

### The Operator Report

This answers *"who holds what, down to the last operator under my cluster?"*:

1. Set your **date range**.
2. Use **Filter by Cluster** to slice to the exact cluster (since you manage one, it's
   already scoped there for you).
3. Read each operator row with their **digital/broadband IDs**, their parent cluster,
   and their **device counts** split by Total / SB / ONT.
4. Use the **Search/Filter Report** bar to jump straight to one operator.

### Efficiency tips

- **Set the date range before anything else** so you never scan All-Time tables.
- **Use SB vs ONT tabs** when investigating a vendor complaint.
- **Search beats scrolling** — for a single operator, use the Filter search instead of
  paging through rows.

## 5. User management

**Purpose:** grow your cluster's field force. As likely the **Cluster's manager**, you can
**create the operators** who report to you and **view their accounts**.

### 5.1 My Users

Open **My Users** from the sidebar (under User Management). The page is titled
**Operator Management** — "View operators in your cluster".

- You see a list of the operators under your cluster, your cluster's stats (total users,
  active/inactive, etc.).
- Click **Add Operator** (top right) to create a new account in your cluster.

### 5.2 Step-by-step: create an operator

1. Open **User Management → My Users**.
2. Click **Add Operator** (the modal is titled **Add New Operator**).
3. In the form give the operator:
   - **Full Name** and **Email**.
   - A **Password** (at least 6 characters) and matching confirm.
4. The system associates the new user with **your cluster** (as their parent) — the
   cluster is fixed for you.
5. (Optional) add **digital / broadband IDs**, and choose a **network name** if needed.
6. Click **Create**. A green message confirms it and the operator appears in your list.

### 5.3 User Hierarchy

Open **User Management → User Hierarchy**. The page is titled **My Operators** —
"Operators in your cluster".

This is a **visual tree** of **You (Cluster) → its Operators**. Search to find one, and
click a node to open its details.

### 5.4 Step-by-step: bulk upload operators

When onboarding several operators at once, use **User Management → Bulk Upload**.

1. Download the **CSV template** and fill one row per operator.
2. Back on the page, choose **Upload Operators** (under your cluster) as the mode.
3. Upload the file. The result screen shows **Created, Warnings, and Errors** — fix any
   error rows and re-upload only those.

### 5.5 Using user management efficiently

- **Create operators under a fixed cluster path** — the parent is pre-set for you, so you
  just fill name/email/password.
- **Bulk upload for onboarding** — this is much faster when adding many operators at once.
- **Mark operators active** — a user must be **active** to log in and work; check their
  status in My Users.

## 6. Devices (My Devices)

**My Devices** shows the devices in your cluster — what you hold plus what your
operators hold.

1. Open **My Devices** from the sidebar.
2. The summary cards at top give your personal totals: **In My Hand**, **Under My Chain**
   (devices your operators hold), **Total in Chain**, **Total Received**, and **Total
   Sent**.
3. Use **Search Devices** with filters by type, cluster, holder, or serial to find one.
4. Click a row (or the **eye** icon) to open **Device Details** — status, holder,
   location, and the full field list. You can **Track Device** from it to see its journey.

> **Why this matters:** you distribute devices from this list. Before creating a
> distribution, move the device here to confirm it is actually with you.

## 7. External Inventory

All external inventory records belong to **PDIC management** — you get a **view-only**
catalog of the items. Open **External Inventory** from the sidebar and you see the
**Items** tab: each row shows the **ID, Name, and Price** of the fixture (OTT boxes,
OLTs, remotes, SB/adapters, and other stocked items). You can search and filter, but you
cannot add, edit, or distribute them — that's management's job.

## 8. The distribution workflow

**Purpose:** distribution is how a device physically moves
**PDIC → Sub Distributor → your Cluster → Operator**. Every distribution is a transfer
that must be **confirmed** by the receiver before it becomes final.

### 8.1 How devices flow to the field

1. **Your distributor (or the Sub Distributor) sends you a distribution**
   (`pending_receipt`).
2. **You receive a notification** warning to confirm the device received.
3. **You confirm or dispute** the delivery (see **Delivery Confirmations**, section 10).
4. Once confirmed, the devices are **yours** — and you **re-distribute to your
   operators** (section 9).

### 8.2 Your part in the workflow

- **Receiver** — the biggest part: confirm each incoming device quickly.
- **Sender** — distribute devices you hold to the operators.
- **You don't create new device records** — you only hand on what has been sent to you
  (PDIC/Sub Distributor hold the authoritative list).
- **Re-distribute** — everything you receive is meant for your operators below you; keep
  moving devices down the chain once confirmed.

### 8.3 The statuses, explained

| Status | What it means | Who acts |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Awaiting Receipt** (`pending_receipt`) | Sent, but the receiver hasn't confirmed it yet. | Receiver confirms or disputes |
| **Confirmed** (`approved`) | Receiver confirmed they got the devices. | Receiver (already done) |
| **Disputed** (`disputed`) | Receiver says the devices did **not** arrive. | You / management |
| Cancelled | The creator cancelled before it was resolved. | Creator |
| Rejected | Management closed the distribution without approving it. | Management |

## 9. Managing distributions

**Purpose:** create and track distributions from your cluster down to your operators.

### 9.1 Step-by-step: create a distribution to an operator

1. Open **Distributions** from the sidebar. Click **Create Distribution** (or
   **Assign to Operator** on your dashboard).
2. On the left, pick the **devices** you want to send:
   - Select filtered (**Select Filtered (N)**), or manual selection one by one.
   - Tip: filter by **status** to select available + in your cluster.
3. **Select Recipient:** pick the **Operator** who is to hold it in your cluster. The
   list is scoped to **your cluster's operators**.
4. Set the **Date of Distribution** (leave blank for today) and any notes.
5. Click **Create Distribution**, review, and **Confirm Transfer**.

### 9.2 Step-by-step: bulk upload a distribution

1. Open **Distributions → Bulk Upload**.
2. **Download the CSV template**: one row per device (`mac_address`, `serial_number`,
   `nuid`).
3. Fill it with your devices, upload the file, and pick the **recipient operator**.
4. Review the summary, then **Upload & Create Distribution**.

### 9.3 Step-by-step: view and analyse your distributions
(after bulk upload, or whenever you need to review recent activity)

1. Open **Distributions**. The cards give a snapshot: **Total, Awaiting Receipt,
   Confirmed, Disputed**.
2. Set the **date range** to focus on a period.
3. **Search** by all fields, distribution ID, from, to, status, confirmed-by, or date.
4. Click a row to open **Distribution Details** — the sender, receiver, device summary,
   status, and **By Device Type / Vendor** breakdown.

### 9.4 Downloading the device list

In the **Distribution details**, click **Download CSV / Excel** to export exactly which
devices (MAC / NUID) were sent in that batch — useful for handing to field staff or
checking what arrived.

### 9.5 Approaching distribution work

- **Always confirm incoming deliveries first** — you can't distribute a device, that is
  still pending receipt.
- **Distribute to the operator that will actually deploy it.** Keep devices moving
  downstream, never pull devices back upward into a distribution to you.
- **Use Bulk Upload when sending sets of devices** — it's faster than picking each one.
- **Keep recipient details accurate** — a correct operator selection means the device is
  tracked to the right person on every screen (Track Device, reports, dues).

## 10. Delivery Confirmations

**Purpose:** this page is where every delivery **to you** gets confirmed or disputed.

### 10.1 Step-by-step: confirm or dispute a delivery

1. Open **Delivery Confirmations** from the sidebar (or use the banner on the dashboard).
2. The list shows each distribution that is **Awaiting Confirmation**:
   - **Confirm Received** — you got it. The devices transfer to you and unlock
     redistribution.
   - **Not Received** — you did **not** receive it (this raises a dispute). Alerts
     management and the sender.
3. Clicking either opens a confirmation modal; you can add a note before submitting.

### 10.2 Step-by-step: confirming from the Distribution details

1. Open the distribution row (or the **Distribution Details**).
2. Click **Confirm Received** at the footer, optionally add a note, and confirm.

> **Why confirm:** until you confirm, the devices are still the sender's, so it's
> essential to confirm every **received** device as soon as it arrives.

## 11. Defect Reports

**Purpose:** when a device fails, the cycle is: someone reports the **defect** → PDIC **reviews**
→ the device is **returned** → a **replacement is assigned** → you **confirm** the
replacement physically arrives.

### 11.1 The defect workflow (read this first)

1. **A field user reports a defect** (you or an operator). The device is marked
   **defective** and management + the sub-distributor are alerted.
2. **Management reviews** — approve or reject.
   - **Approve** → a **return request** is auto-created, telling the holder to return the
     device.
   - **Reject** → the report is closed.
3. **The device comes back** — management confirms it arrived at PDIC. Return **received**.
4. **A replacement is assigned** only after the return is **received**.
5. **You confirm the replacement** (section 11.3) → the report becomes resolved and the
   new device is active with you.

### 11.2 Step-by-step: report a defect

1. Open **Defect Reports** and click **Report Defect** (top right).
2. Pick the **device** (search by model / serial / NUID).
3. Add the **description**, **severity**, and (optional) **photos**.
4. Submit. The device is marked defective and management is notified to review it.

### 11.3 Step-by-step: confirm a replacement receipt

1. Open **Defect Reports**.
2. A replacement pending **receipt** — shows a **Confirm** button.
3. Press **Confirm** **only after physically receiving** the device. The report becomes
   **resolved** and the device activates in your account.

### 11.4 The Defect Reports page, in detail

- **Summary cards** — Total, Open, Under Review, Resolved, Critical.
- **Search bar** with fields (serial, NUID, report ID, severity, status, reported-by,
  etc.).
- **Table** with device, severity, reported-by, date, status, **payment status**.
- **Pending Defective Device Return** and **Replacement Assignment Queue** panels show
  what's waiting in your area.

## 12. Pending Payments & Replacements

### 12.1 The Pending Payments page

**Purpose:** lists **money owed** by your operators (or field users) for returned
defective devices.

1. Open **Pending Payments** from the sidebar.
2. Summary cards: **Users, Total Due, Total Items**.
3. The table groups dues **per user**: name, role, parent, digital/broad ID, number of
   due items, total due (highest first). Search by user, parent, or IDs.
4. Open a user row for a **details drawer** of each defect with its return amount and
   received date.

> **Your action:** this view keeps you aware of dues in your cluster. Payment
> confirmation happens at PDIC/Management; your job here is to **view** and follow up.

### 12.2 The Replacements pages

**Purpose:** Replacement records link a **defective device** to its **replacement
mapping**. The **Replacements** menu page appears when there are **received returns**.

1. **Replacements** — the list of defective→replacement mappings; search and open rows.
2. **Pending Replacements** (`/replacements/pending`) — splits approved defects into
   **awaiting / ready / waiting-for-shipment** counts, so you see if a replacement for
   your area is waiting to ship.

### 12.3 Replacement Confirmation

When a replacement leading to you is ready, the flow closes on **Replacement
Confirmation** (and the **Confirm** button on Defect Reports):

1. Open **Replacement Confirmation** from the sidebar.
2. Open the replacement waiting for you.
3. **Confirm only after the device physically arrives**. The replacement becomes active in
   your account and the defect is marked resolved.
